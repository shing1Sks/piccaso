# 01 — Prior work: Stroke-based rendering and neural painting

Survey date: 2026-10-03. Scope: image → strokes (stroke-based rendering / neural painting), and prompt/class/noise → strokes (generative painting). The target project is a **tiny (10–100M) model that emits parametric brush strokes (curve + width + color + alpha) for a fixed, non-learned rasterizer**, conditioned on text or class, with broad coverage.

**How this was verified.** Each entry was checked against the arXiv PDF text, the official project/GitHub page, or the GitHub API (for licenses and star counts). Anything I could not confirm from a primary source is marked **(unverified)**. "Speed" numbers are the authors' own figures on the hardware they name. They are not comparable across papers unless they come from the same table.

Abbreviations: **Recon** = needs an input/target image. **Gen** = generates from noise, class, or text. **NR** = neural (learned) stroke renderer. **Fixed** = non-learned or analytic renderer.

## 2. Notes by family

**A. Heuristic SBR (Hertzmann 1998, Im2Oil).** These need no learning, run on CPU, and give an inherently coarse-to-fine order (largest brush first). Hertzmann's long curved B-spline strokes are the "painterly look" baseline. Their weakness is many strokes and no semantics.

**B. RL / sequential agents (SPIRAL, SPIRAL++, StrokeNet, Learning to Paint, Intelli-Paint, CNP).**
- SPIRAL/SPIRAL++ are **the only ones here that were trained as unconditional generators**. They paint faces and single ImageNet classes from noise with 17–1000 strokes. SPIRAL++ shows that **abstraction emerges with short stroke budgets (17–32 strokes)**, which is directly relevant to a "few-strokes painterly" target.
- Learning to Paint is the canonical recon model. Its 13-param quadratic Bézier with width and alpha at both ends is essentially the parameterization this project wants.
- RL training is unstable and slow (PT's table: about 40 h for LTP versus under 4 h for PT).

**C. Optimization (SNP, Kotovenko et al., CLIPDraw/StyleCLIPDraw/CLIPasso/FRIDA, LIVE/VectorFusion/SVGDreamer/VectorPainter, Birth of a Painting).**
- These give the best quality per stroke but take minutes per image on a GPU (SNP 76–521 s; CLIPasso ~6 min).
- The text-driven ones (CLIPDraw, VectorFusion, SVGDreamer, FRIDA) prove that **text → strokes is possible**, but only through **per-prompt test-time optimization against huge frozen models** (CLIP/SD). None is an amortized small generator.
- SNP's **optimal-transport loss** and Birth-of-a-Painting's **dual-color strokes + smudge** are the strongest quality tricks.

**D. Feed-forward predictors (Paint Transformer, AttentionPainter, MambaPainter, DQ-Transformer).** These are fast (0.07–0.7 s per image on GPU), small (~9–25M), and suitable for bulk dataset conversion. PT is trained purely on synthetic strokes, so it has no domain bias. MambaPainter is trained on ImageNet.

**E. Stroke-space generative models (the closest to this project).**
- **Collaborative Neural Painting** (class-conditional stroke diffusion, 10 classes, 400 × 8-param strokes).
- **AttentionPainter's Stroke Diffusion Model** (stroke-space diffusion for inpainting/editing, per domain).
- **PaintCopilot** (flow-matching next-stroke model plus label-conditioned region cVAE, portraits).
- **SPIRAL++** (unconditional RL generator).
- In the vector-icon domain: **SVGFusion**, **StrokeNUWA**, and the **SIGGRAPH 2025 path-level T2V diffusion** are feed-forward text-conditioned generators, but they output flat-fill icon/clipart paths, not brushstrokes, and are trained on human SVGs.

**F. Painting-process video models (ProcessPainter, Inverse Painting, Paints-Undo, AnimatePainter, Loomis Painter).** These produce pixel frames, not parametric strokes. They are large (SD-scale), slow (Paints-Undo takes 5–10 min per image on a 24 GB GPU), and are **not** stroke generators. They matter only as a source of "human-like ordering" ideas.

## 3. Conclusions

### 3.1 The gap: has anyone built a small text- or class-conditioned generator of brush-stroke sequences for broad categories?

**As far as I can find, no published work does all of these at once:**
(a) amortized feed-forward or autoregressive generation, with no per-prompt optimization;
(b) text or many-class conditioning with broad coverage;
(c) parametric *brush* strokes for a fixed rasterizer;
(d) model size of 10–100M.

What comes closest, and what each one is missing:

| Closest work | What it has | What it lacks for this project |
|---|---|---|
| **Collaborative Neural Painting** (CVIU 2025) | Class-conditional diffusion transformer in **8-param stroke space**. 400 strokes. ~0.56 s per sample on A100. Plausibly 10–100M params (unverified). **Data pipeline: SD-generated images → object segmentation → SNP decomposition (101k sequences)**. | Only **10 animal classes**, isolated objects on blank backgrounds, no text conditioning ("incorporating language models to enable open-set semantics" is listed as *future work*). Dataset not yet released. |
| **AttentionPainter SDM** (TVCG 2025) | Diffusion directly on stroke parameters (8- or 13-param strokes). Fast stroke predictor used as an "encoder". | Per-domain (faces, birds, cars). Conditioned on masked image or sketch, not on class or text. |
| **PaintCopilot** (2026) | Autoregressive flow-matching next-stroke transformer + label-conditioned region generator. 8-dim strokes. | Portraits only (3k paintings). Continuation and co-creation rather than prompt → full painting. |
| **SPIRAL / SPIRAL++** (2018/19) | Generates *from noise* in stroke space. Shows abstraction with 17–32 strokes. Includes per-class ImageNet agents. | One agent per dataset or class, no text, RL+GAN training is hard to scale, 64×64. |
| **CLIPDraw / VectorFusion / SVGDreamer / FRIDA / VectorPainter** | Text → strokes for arbitrary prompts. | Per-prompt optimization (minutes on GPU) through CLIP or SD (~0.4–1B+). Not a small model. |
| **SVGFusion / StrokeNUWA / path-level T2V (SIGGRAPH 2025)** | Feed-forward text → vector primitives. | Icon/clipart flat fills from human SVG data, not painterly brushstrokes. LLM-sized in some cases. |
| **Sketch-RNN, SketchAgent, VQ-SGen** | Class- or text-conditioned stroke sequences. | Line sketches only (no color, width, or paint). SketchAgent needs a frontier LLM. |

**Bottom line.** The ingredients are all published separately:
- stroke-space diffusion (CNP-collab, AttentionPainter);
- autoregressive next-stroke flow matching (PaintCopilot);
- fast image → stroke encoders (PT, MambaPainter, AttentionPainter);
- a scalable synthetic-data recipe (text-to-image model → decomposer).

No one has scaled them to **open-vocabulary text or ImageNet-scale classes with a small model**. The single most direct precedent to build on, and to compare against, is **Collaborative Neural Painting**. My claim of novelty is limited to what is findable. Unpublished or non-English work may exist.

**Recommendation.** Bulk-convert with **Paint Transformer**, or with **MambaPainter** if you are on a CUDA GPU and want quality. Cap the scales (K ≈ 3–4) so each image has ~150–700 strokes, and keep strokes ordered by scale, then by size. Optionally refine a subset with a short SNP-style differentiable optimization (OT + L1 loss) through *your own* fixed rasterizer, so the stored parameters are exactly consistent with the runtime renderer.

Also note the **CNP-collab recipe**: generate images with a T2I model, so text and class labels come for free, and then decompose them. This sidesteps captioning and licensing issues with real photos.

### 3.3 Stroke parameterizations that look painterly with few strokes

1. **Textured oil-brush stamp with affine parameters: `(x, y, w, h, θ, r, g, b)`, 8 params** (PT, CNP, MambaPainter, AttentionPainter, SNP "oil", CNP-collab, PaintCopilot's 8-dim).
   - Rendering is a trivial, non-learned affine warp of one brush texture plus alpha. It is cheap on CPU or phone.
   - The texture does most of the "painted" work, which is why PT and SNP results look like paintings even at a few hundred strokes.
   - This is the most proven format for *generative* stroke models (CNP-collab, PaintCopilot, AttentionPainter SDM).
   - **Upgrade:** SNP's *two colors (head/tail) = 11 params*, or Birth-of-a-Painting's dual-color strokes, give in-stroke gradients that look much better at low counts.
2. **Quadratic Bézier + per-end width + per-end alpha + RGB: 13 params** (Learning to Paint, AttentionPainter Bézier variant; SNP watercolor is the 15-param version with two colors).
   - Curved strokes capture contours and gestures with fewer primitives. It rasterizes analytically (stamp circles along the curve), so DiffVG or a custom stamp renderer works.
   - Without a texture it looks smoother and more "airbrushed". Adding a brush-texture stamp along the curve gives the painterly look.
3. **Long curved B-spline / Catmull-Rom strokes with constant color** (Hertzmann; StrokeNet with 16 anchors).
   - These are the most expressive per stroke, but variable length is awkward for fixed-size tokens. Resampling to 3–4 control points fixes this.
4. **2D Gaussians** (PaintCopilot's Gaussian brush; Image-GS). About 8 params, smooth gradients, very optimizer-friendly, but the result looks soft rather than "brushy".

**Few-stroke evidence:**
- SPIRAL++: recognizable faces in 17–32 strokes.
- CLIPDraw: 16 strokes gives a cartoonish but recognizable result, 256 is detailed.
- Intelli-Paint: 60–80% fewer strokes by regularization.
- SNP tape art: minimal strokes.
- CNP-collab: 400 strokes is enough for a class-conditional generative model.

**Practical pick for this project:** 8–11-param textured oil strokes (optionally with alpha) as the primary token, or a 13-param Bézier + width + alpha if curved gestures matter. Use a coarse-to-fine budget of roughly 64–400 strokes and a fixed stamp renderer shared by the data converter and the runtime.
